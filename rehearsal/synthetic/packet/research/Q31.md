# Q31. Can an adult sibling of the founder, paid for services, be the independent co-trustee, or be paid by the Foundation or the operating company?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice. Synthetic: the person is "an adult sibling of the founder, paid for services"; no names or family details.

- Question: Can an adult sibling of the founder serve as the independent trustee route R2 counts on; can the Foundation pay that sibling for services without self-dealing; what must an operating company show to deduct the pay; and does naming the sibling a beneficiary move any tax?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: likely
- Lawyer decides: Whether the sibling serves in any trustee or officer role, whether the sibling is a substantial contributor or a foundation manager (which makes the sibling a disqualified person), what the services are and what market pay is, and how the sibling is classified (employee or contractor).
- Claims:
  1. A brother or sister of the grantor is a related or subordinate party, presumed subservient to the grantor for sections 674 and 675 unless shown otherwise.
     - Trace: IRC 672(c)(2) | "The grantor’s father, mother, issue, brother or sister" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/672 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 672(c) | "a related or subordinate party shall be presumed to be subservient to the grantor" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/672 (read 2026-11-25)
  2. The 674(c) exception for independent trustees limits related or subordinate parties to half of the trustees and requires the power to be exercised without anyone else's approval. A sole sibling trustee, or a sibling paired with the founder, fails it; a sibling is at most the minority of a board whose majority is unrelated.
     - Trace: IRC 674(c) | "none of whom is the grantor, and no more than half of whom are related or subordinate parties who are subservient to the wishes of the grantor" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/674 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 674(c) | "solely exercisable (without the approval or consent of any other person)" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/674 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Claim for R2: a sibling cannot be the independent trustee; the independent trustee must be someone else (see Q30).
  3. For the Foundation, a sibling is not in the family list of 4946(d).
     - Trace: IRC 4946(d) | "the family of any individual shall include only his spouse, ancestors, children, grandchildren, great grandchildren, and the spouses of children, grandchildren, and great grandchildren" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4946 (read 2026-11-25)
  4. A sibling is a disqualified person only by another route: substantial contributor, foundation manager (officer, director or trustee), or an entity in which those persons own more than 35 percent.
     - Trace: IRC 4946(a)(1)(A), (B) | "a substantial contributor to the foundation, (B) a foundation manager (within the meaning of subsection (b)(1))" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4946 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4946(b)(1) | "an officer, director, or trustee of a foundation" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4946 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4946(a)(1)(E) | "a corporation of which persons described in subparagraph (A), (B), (C), or (D) own more than 35 percent of the total combined voting power" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4946 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Reading (not a quote): if the sibling is none of these, the payment is not between the foundation and a disqualified person, so 4941(d)(1)(D) does not reach it; the sibling is not a disqualified person by family. Not found: whether a sibling who is a founder-relative but not in 4946(d) is caught by any other 4941 rule (none read).
  5. If the sibling is a disqualified person, 4941(d)(1)(D) makes pay a self-dealing act, and 4941(d)(2)(E) excepts personal-services pay that is reasonable, necessary and not excessive.
     - Trace: IRC 4941(d)(1)(D) | "payment of compensation (or payment or reimbursement of expenses) by a private foundation to a disqualified person" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4941 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4941(d)(2)(E) | "personal services which are reasonable and necessary to carrying out the exempt purpose of the private foundation shall not be an act of self-dealing if the compensation (or payment or reimbursement) is not excessive" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4941 (read 2026-11-25)
  6. Check against indirect benefit to the founder: use of foundation income or assets for the benefit of a disqualified person is self-dealing, so pay that in substance serves the founder fails even if the sibling is not disqualified.
     - Trace: IRC 4941(d)(1)(E) | "transfer to, or use by or for the benefit of, a disqualified person of the income or assets of a private foundation" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4941 (read 2026-11-25)
  7. Check that the work serves the Foundation's purpose: an amount paid for a purpose other than a charitable one is a taxable expenditure.
     - Trace: IRC 4945(d)(5) | "for any purpose other than one specified in section 170(c)(2)(B)" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4945 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4945(d) | "the term “taxable expenditure” means any amount paid or incurred by a private foundation" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4945 (read 2026-11-25)
  8. Operating-company pay is deductible only if reasonable and for services actually rendered, measured against like enterprises.
     - Trace: 26 CFR 1.162-7(a) | "a reasonable allowance for salaries or other compensation for personal services actually rendered" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.162-7 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 1.162-7(b)(3) | "such amount as would ordinarily be paid for like services by like enterprises under like circumstances" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.162-7 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 1.162-7(b)(3) | "those existing at the date when the contract for services was made, not those existing at the date when the contract is questioned" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.162-7 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Checks as lines: (a) real work done; (b) market pay with a written benchmark made when the contract is signed; (c) records kept: agreement, timesheets or deliverables, invoices or payroll; (d) right worker classification (employee or contractor). Line (d) is a checklist item with no authority read in this run.
  9. Naming a sibling a beneficiary moves tax only if the money is really his: income is taxed to the beneficiary to whom it is required to be distributed, or paid, credited or required to be distributed.
     - Trace: IRC 652(a) | "shall be included in the gross income of the beneficiaries to whom the income is required to be distributed, whether distributed or not" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/652 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 662(a) | "there shall be included in the gross income of a beneficiary to whom an amount specified in section 661(a) is paid, credited, or required to be distributed" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/662 (read 2026-11-25)
