# Q10. How do distributions for minor children stay separate from his support duties?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: If the trust pays for things the founder is legally obliged to provide any minor children, that income is taxed to him (677(b)). How should the document keep distributions to them separate from his support duties?
- Packet: A
- Coverage flag: none
- Confidence: likely
- Lawyer decides: How the instrument words the trustee's power so that distributions never discharge his legal support duty, and whether his state law treats a given expense as support.
- Claims:
  1. Trust income is not taxed to the grantor merely because another person, or the grantor as trustee, may use it for support of a beneficiary the grantor must support.
     - Trace: IRC 677(b) | "Income of a trust shall not be considered taxable to the grantor under subsection (a) or any other provision of this chapter merely because such income in the discretion of another person" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/677 (read 2026-11-25)
  2. The exception stops to the extent the income is actually applied or distributed for that support.
     - Trace: IRC 677(b) | "whom the grantor is legally obligated to support or maintain, except to the extent that such income is so applied or distributed." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/677 (read 2026-11-25)
  3. What counts as a legal support obligation is set by state law, not by the Code text read here.
     - Trace: no authority found | searched: 26 USC 677 on law.cornell.edu for a definition of the support obligation (read 2026-11-25)
