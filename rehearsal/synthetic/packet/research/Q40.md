# Q40. For each promise in the old trust paperwork, what is the lawful way to deliver it, and who provides it?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice. SYNTHETIC: no real figures, names or clause text; the old instruments' wording stays in private notes. Roles, not names.

- Question: The old, unsigned and unfunded trust drafts promised the trustee housing, meals, vehicles, fuel and travel, food, clothing, education, healthcare and retirement, plus privacy, protection from creditors, their own courts and venue, untaxed units, the power to set taxes or money, and funding by exchange. Which of these has a lawful route, through which entity, and which has none?
- Packet: A
- Coverage flag: the benefits come through an operating or management company, which could read as a business or investment interest.
- Confidence: likely
- Assumes: the management company is a C corporation that employs the founder and pays him reasonable compensation (Q34); the Family Trust is a domestic trust with an independent distribution trustee (Q4, Q39).
- Lawyer decides: Which of the routes below the instruments and the company's plans adopt, and how the trust's own terms (spendthrift clause, governing law, place of administration, certification of trust) are drafted. The rule under all of them: personal benefits come from the company as an employer, under the closed list of exclusions, never from the Foundation or the Family Trust's charitable share, and the trust itself never pays the trustee's living costs.
- Claims:
  1. Housing and meals: the company can exclude them only if furnished for its convenience on its business premises (lodging also as a condition of employment). Paid by a trust, they are personal living expenses with no deduction. Route: company, narrow. Trust: no route.
     - Trace: IRC 119(a) | "for the convenience of the employer, but only if" ... "the meals are furnished on the business premises of the employer" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/119 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 262(a) | "no deduction shall be allowed for personal, living, or family expenses." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/262 (read 2026-11-26)
  2. Vehicles, fuel and travel: business use only, as a working condition fringe the founder could have deducted himself, or as reimbursement under an accountable plan that requires substantiation and the return of any excess. Personal miles are pay. Route: company.
     - Trace: IRC 132(d) | "such payment would be allowable as a deduction under section 162 or 167." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/132 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 62(c) | "such arrangement does not require the employee to substantiate the expenses covered by the arrangement" ... "such arrangement provides the employee the right to retain any amount in excess of the substantiated expenses" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/62 (read 2026-11-26)
  3. Healthcare: employer-provided health coverage is excluded from an employee's income, the founder included when the company is a C corporation; a self-insured reimbursement plan has owner limits. Route: company.
     - Trace: IRC 106(a) | "gross income of an employee does not include employer-provided coverage under an accident or health plan." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/106 (read 2026-11-25)
  4. Education: up to 5,250 a year under an educational assistance plan, but no more than 5 percent of the plan's cost may go to owners, so it fails while the founder is the only employee. Route: company, once it has other employees.
     - Trace: IRC 127(a)(2) | "this section shall apply only to the first $5,250 of such assistance so furnished." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/127 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 127(b)(3) | "Not more than 5 percent of the amounts paid or incurred by the employer" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/127 (read 2026-11-25)
  5. Retirement: a qualified plan whose trust is a U.S. trust for the exclusive benefit of the employees, such as a one-participant 401(k). Route: company sponsors it.
     - Trace: IRC 401(a) | "A trust created or organized in the United States and" ... "forming part of a stock bonus, pension, or profit-sharing plan of an employer" ... "for the exclusive benefit of his employees or their beneficiaries" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/401 (read 2026-11-26)
  6. Food and clothing for daily life: no route. They are personal living expenses whoever pays them; work clothing that is not fit for ordinary wear was not researched.
     - Trace: IRC 262(a) | "Except as otherwise expressly provided in this chapter," | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/262 (read 2026-11-26)
  7. Nothing on this list may come from the Foundation or a trust share set aside for it: using its income or assets for a disqualified person is self-dealing (Q34 claim 5).
     - Trace: IRC 4941(d)(1)(E) | "transfer to, or use by or for the benefit of, a disqualified person of the income or assets of a private foundation" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4941 (read 2026-11-25)
  8. Protection from creditors: a spendthrift clause that bars both voluntary and involuntary transfer keeps a beneficiary's creditors from the trust interest until it is paid out. Route: the Family Trust's own terms.
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/502(a) | "A spendthrift provision is valid only if it prohibits both voluntary and involuntary transfer of a beneficiary's interest." | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/502(c) | "a creditor or assignee of the beneficiary may not reach the interest or a distribution by the trustee before its receipt by the beneficiary." | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
  9. Privacy: lawful privacy is from outsiders, not from beneficiaries. A certification of trust lets the trustee prove the trust and his powers to a bank or title company without showing its terms; but the trustee must tell each qualified beneficiary the trust exists and of the right to a copy. Route: certification of trust. A secrecy clause against beneficiaries: no route. An Illinois land trust for title privacy was not researched.
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/1013(a) | "Instead of furnishing a copy of the trust instrument to a person other than a beneficiary, the trustee may furnish to the person a certification of trust" | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/1013(d) | "A certification of trust need not contain the dispositive terms of a trust." | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/813.1(b)(1) | "The trustee shall notify each qualified beneficiary:" ... "of the trust's existence;" | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
  10. Courts and venue: the instrument may pick its governing law and principal place of administration, and that choice is honored. Route: the Family Trust's own terms. Refusing a court's authority: no route.
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/107(b) | "the laws of this State govern the administration of a trust while the principal place of administration is this State." | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/108(a) | "the terms of a trust designating the principal place of administration are valid and controlling if:" | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
  11. Funding: the lawful routes are a gift (reported for gift tax) or a sale at full value. A transfer for less than full value is a gift to the extent of the difference, whatever the paper calls it. Route: a documented gift or sale.
     - Trace: IRC 2512(b) | "Where property is transferred for less than an adequate and full consideration in money or money's worth" ... "shall be deemed a gift" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/2512 (read 2026-11-26)
  12. Untaxed units of beneficial interest, and a trust that sets its own taxes or decides what money is: no route. Income is taxed under the Code to the trust, its grantor or its beneficiaries, and a trust running a business is taxed as a business entity (Q39 claim 8).
     - Trace: no authority found | searched: IRC 641, 671 to 679 and 26 CFR 301.7701-4 for any rule letting an instrument exempt its own units or set its own taxes; none found (read 2026-11-26)
  13. Closing out the old paperwork: an EIN cannot be cancelled, only deactivated, by a letter giving the entity's EIN, legal name, address, assignment notice and reason, after any returns owed are filed. Whether creating a foreign trust with no transfer was itself a reportable event (the creation of a foreign trust is one), and whether an unsigned instrument was ever created, are for counsel.
     - Trace: IRS, If you no longer need your EIN | "we can’t cancel it, but we can deactivate it." | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/if-you-no-longer-need-your-ein (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: IRS, If you no longer need your EIN | "Send us a letter that includes your entity’s EIN, legal name, address, EIN assignment notice, if available" | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/if-you-no-longer-need-your-ein (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: IRC 6048(a)(3)(A) | "the creation of any foreign trust by a United States person" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6048 (read 2026-11-26)
