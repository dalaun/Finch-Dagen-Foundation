# Q13. What must the Family Trust have before it can hold title to an asset?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: For a non-grantor Family Trust under route R2 or R3, what must exist before it can take title to an income-producing asset: instrument terms, trustee acceptance, EIN, Illinois situs, bank account, and who signs for the trust?
- Packet: A
- Coverage flag: the trust would later hold LLC interests in a business or rental, which could read as a business or investment interest.
- Confidence: open
- Assumes: Illinois situs for the Family Trust (see Q22)
- Lawyer decides: What the Illinois instrument, trustee acceptance and signing authority must contain; the federal EIN step is traced below.
- Claims:
  1. The trust needs its own EIN (the IRS lists trusts among entities that need one).
     - Trace: IRS, Employer ID Numbers | "You also need an EIN to operate any of these entities:" | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/employer-id-numbers (read 2026-11-25)
  2. Anyone required to furnish an EIN must apply for one.
     - Trace: Treas. Reg. 301.6109-1(d)(2)(i) | "Any person required to furnish an employer identification number must apply for one" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/301.6109-1 (read 2026-11-25)
  3. Illinois creates a trust only if the settlor has capacity and shows the intent, the trust has a definite beneficiary (or is charitable), the trustee has duties, and the same person is not both sole trustee and sole beneficiary.
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/402(a) | "the settlor or other person creating the trust indicates an intention to create the trust" | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/402(a) | "the trust has a definite beneficiary or is:" | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/402(a) | "the same person is not the sole trustee and sole beneficiary." | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
  4. The trustee accepts by the method the instrument sets, or, failing that, by taking the property, acting as trustee or otherwise showing acceptance; silence for 120 days after notice is a decline. So the trustee's signed acceptance should come before the trust takes title.
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/701(a) | "by substantially complying with a method of acceptance provided in the trust instrument" | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/701(b) | "A designated trustee who does not accept the trusteeship within 120 days after receiving notice of the designation is deemed to have declined the trusteeship." | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
  5. Situs: the instrument's choice of a principal place of administration holds if a trustee lives or does business there or any part of the administration happens there, and Illinois law governs administration while that place is Illinois.
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/108(a) | "the terms of a trust designating the principal place of administration are valid and controlling if:" | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/107(b) | "the laws of this State govern the administration of a trust while the principal place of administration is this State." | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
  6. An Illinois trust is irrevocable unless the instrument expressly makes it revocable.
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/602(a) | "The settlor may revoke a trust only if the trust instrument expressly provides that the trust is revocable or that the settlor has an unrestricted power of amendment." | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
