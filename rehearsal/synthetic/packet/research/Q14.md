# Q14. What other income will the two adult beneficiaries have, so their own tax brackets are known?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: Roughly what other yearly income will the two adult beneficiaries have, and at what level does a trust distribution to them cost more tax than it saves?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: open
- Lawyer decides: The beneficiaries' real other income is a fact for Dalaun and the CPA; the rules below only say that distributed income is taxed to them.
- Claims:
  1. Income a simple trust must distribute currently is included in the beneficiaries' gross income whether or not it is distributed.
     - Trace: IRC 652(a) | "shall be included in the gross income of the beneficiaries to whom the income is required to be distributed, whether distributed or not" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/652 (read 2026-11-25)
  2. For a complex trust, the amount of income required to be distributed currently, and other amounts paid, is included in the beneficiary's gross income.
     - Trace: IRC 662(a) | "there shall be included in the gross income of a beneficiary to whom an amount specified in section 661(a) is paid, credited, or required to be distributed" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/662 (read 2026-11-25)
  3. A trust's retained income is taxed to the trust on its own rate table rather than the beneficiaries' (the statute's base table is shown; current dollar thresholds are inflation-adjusted and not read here).
     - Trace: IRC 1(e) | "There is hereby imposed on the taxable income of" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1 (read 2026-11-25)
