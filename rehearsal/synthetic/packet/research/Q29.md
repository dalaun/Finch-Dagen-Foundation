# Q29. If the beneficiaries are minors, does holding the asset in the Family Trust still make sense?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice. SYNTHETIC: one hypothetical minor beneficiary; no real family details.

- Question: With minor beneficiaries, income above the kiddie-tax steps is taxed at the founder's rate (IRC 1(g)), so the trust saves no income tax by splitting. Is there a reason beyond control and creditor protection of a minor's assets, succession if the founder dies or is disabled, and the Foundation route to use the trust, and how should the instrument handle the age at which the kiddie tax stops (19, or 24 for a full-time student)?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: likely
- Lawyer decides: Whether a trust-law reason (control, creditor protection, succession) justifies the trust's yearly cost while the beneficiaries are minors, and how the instrument should time distributions around the ages at which the kiddie tax ends.
- Claims:
  1. Section 1(g) applies to a child's unearned income; the child's tax is the greater of the ordinary tax or the tax on income reduced by the child's net unearned income, plus the child's share of the allocable parental tax.
     - Trace: IRC 1(g)(1) | "such child’s share of the allocable parental tax" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 1(g)(1) | "the tax imposed by this section shall be equal to the greater of" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1 (read 2026-11-25)
  2. The parental tax is the extra tax the parent would owe if the children's net unearned income were added to the parent's taxable income. This is why a minor's share saves nothing at the parent's bracket.
     - Trace: IRC 1(g)(3)(A) | "the tax which would be imposed by this section on the parent’s taxable income if such income included the net unearned income of all children of the parent to whom this subsection applies" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1 (read 2026-11-25)
  3. The rule covers a child under 18 at year end, and an 18-year-old (or older student) whose earned income does not exceed half of the child's own support, if the child meets the age requirements of 152(c)(3).
     - Trace: IRC 1(g)(2)(A)(i) | "has not attained age 18 before the close of the taxable year" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 1(g)(2)(A)(ii)(I) | "has attained age 18 before the close of the taxable year and meets the age requirements of section 152(c)(3)" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1 (read 2026-11-25)
  4. Under 152(c)(3) the ages are under 19, or a student under 24, at the close of the calendar year. These are the ages the instrument would time distributions around.
     - Trace: IRC 152(c)(3)(A) | "is a student who has not attained the age of 24 as of the close of such calendar year" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/152 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 152(c)(3)(A)(i) | "has not attained the age of 19 as of the close of the calendar year in which the taxable year of the taxpayer begins" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/152 (read 2026-11-25)
  5. Net unearned income is the child's unearned adjusted gross income above a floor tied to the standard deduction for dependents (the dollar step is in Rev. Proc. 2025-32, a PDF the machine check cannot read, so it is not traced here).
     - Trace: IRC 1(g)(4)(A) | "the portion of the adjusted gross income for the taxable year which is not attributable to earned income" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1 (read 2026-11-25)
  6. A parental election to report the child's interest and dividends on the parent's return exists, but it is limited to a child whose gross income is only interest and dividends, so it would not reach rental income.
     - Trace: IRC 1(g)(7)(A)(i) | "has gross income for the taxable year only from interest and dividends" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1 (read 2026-11-25)
  7. Any reason, beyond tax, to hold the asset in the trust while the beneficiaries are minors (control, creditor protection, succession, guardianship).
