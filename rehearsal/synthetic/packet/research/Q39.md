# Q39. Is each trust in the stack a domestic trust, and if any is foreign, who is taxed on it and what must be filed?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice. SYNTHETIC: no real figures, names, places or account details. Roles, not names.

- Question: Is each instrument a trust for tax purposes at all, and does each trust pass both United States-person tests (a U.S. court supervises it, and U.S. persons control every substantial decision)? For any trust that fails, is a U.S. founder who funded it taxed as its owner, and which notices and returns are due?
- Packet: A
- Coverage flag: none
- Confidence: likely
- Assumes: the Family Trust is meant to be a domestic, non-grantor trust with the founder as a U.S. trustee and U.S. family members as beneficiaries.
- Lawyer decides: For each trust in the stack, especially any that names a foreign jurisdiction's law or was given a foreign-entity tax number, whether it passes the court test and the control test today; if it fails, whether to convert it to a domestic trust or start over with a domestic instrument, and what notices and returns are owed for any years it already held property. Also whether any instrument is a business entity, not a trust (claim 8), and whether any fails the red-flag checks (claim 10), in which case it is not built on. A trust that fails either test is foreign, and a foreign trust funded by a U.S. person with a U.S. beneficiary is that person's grantor trust, so a "non-grantor" label on it does not hold.
- Claims:
  1. A trust is a United States person only if it meets both tests on the same day: a U.S. court can exercise primary supervision over its administration (court test), and U.S. persons control all of its substantial decisions (control test).
     - Trace: IRC 7701(a)(30)(E) | "a court within the United States is able to exercise primary supervision over the administration of the trust" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/7701 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: IRC 7701(a)(30)(E) | "have the authority to control all substantial decisions of the trust." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/7701 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 301.7701-7(a)(2) | "on any day that the trust meets both the court test and the control test." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/301.7701-7 (read 2026-11-26)
  2. Any trust that fails either test is a foreign trust. A U.S. trustee is not enough by itself.
     - Trace: IRC 7701(a)(31)(B) | "any trust other than a trust described in subparagraph (E) of paragraph (30)" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/7701 (read 2026-11-26)
  3. The control test fails if anyone other than U.S. persons can make or veto a substantial decision, so a foreign protector, co-trustee or committee member with a decision or a veto makes the trust foreign even with a Chicago trustee.
     - Trace: 26 CFR 301.7701-7(d)(1)(iii) | "having the power, by vote or otherwise, to make all of the substantial decisions of the trust" ... "with no other person having the power to veto any of the substantial decisions." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/301.7701-7 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 301.7701-7(d)(4), Example 4 | "Because A, a nonresident alien, controls a substantial decision, the control test is not satisfied." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/301.7701-7 (read 2026-11-26)
  4. A U.S. person who transfers property to a foreign trust, directly or through someone else, is taxed as its owner for any year the trust has a U.S. beneficiary, and a foreign trust is presumed to have one unless it shows otherwise. Grade: a foreign trust funded by the founder with U.S. family beneficiaries is his grantor trust, whatever its paperwork calls it.
     - Trace: IRC 679(a)(1) | "A United States person who directly or indirectly transfers property to a foreign trust" ... "if for such year there is a United States beneficiary of any portion of such trust." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/679 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: IRC 679(c)(1) | "a trust shall be treated as having a United States beneficiary for the taxable year unless" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/679 (read 2026-11-26)
  5. Creating a foreign trust, or transferring money or property to one, is a reportable event that requires notice to the IRS (Form 3520), and a U.S. owner must see that the trust files a full yearly accounting (Form 3520-A).
     - Trace: IRC 6048(a)(3)(A) | "the creation of any foreign trust by a United States person" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6048 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: IRC 6048(b)(1) | "such trust makes a return for such year which sets forth a full and complete accounting" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6048 (read 2026-11-26)
  6. A missed notice costs the greater of $10,000 or 35 percent of the amount that should have been reported; for the owner's yearly trust return the rate is 5 percent, and the owner is liable.
     - Trace: IRC 6677(a) | "shall pay a penalty equal to the greater of $10,000 or 35 percent of the gross reportable amount." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6677 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: IRC 6677(b)(1) | "shall be liable for the penalty imposed by subsection (a)" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6677 (read 2026-11-26)
  7. Whether a trust that already failed a test can be cured by moving its supervision and every substantial decision to U.S. persons, and how the years before that are reported, were not read; that is for counsel.
     - Trace: no authority found | searched: 26 CFR 301.7701-7 (c) and (d) as read on 2026-11-26 for a conversion rule; IRC 6048 and 6677 (read 2026-11-26)
  8. A trust that is a device to carry on a profit-making business is not a trust for tax purposes; it is classified as a business entity (a corporation, partnership or sole proprietorship), and if the grantor keeps control the grantor trust rules apply. Calling it a trust, or a "common law" trust, does not change that.
     - Trace: 26 CFR 301.7701-4(b) | "which are not classified as trusts for purposes of the Internal Revenue Code" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/301.7701-4 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 301.7701-4(b) | "simply as a device to carry on a profit-making business" ... "which normally would have been carried on through business organizations" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/301.7701-4 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: IRS, Abusive Trust Tax Evasion Schemes: Special Types of Trusts | "The regulations require that trusts operating a trade or business be treated as a corporation," ... "partnership, or sole proprietorship, if the grantor, beneficiary or fiduciary materially participates" | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/abusive-trust-tax-evasion-schemes-special-types-of-trusts (read 2026-11-26)
  9. A trust that pays its trustee's or family's personal living costs (housing, food, vehicles, education) gets no deduction for them, and a grantor who keeps powers or benefits is taxed on all of the trust's income.
     - Trace: IRS, Abusive Trust Tax Evasion Schemes: Questions and Answers | "personal living expenses such as food, utilities, recreational expenses, children's education" ... "are not allowed as a trust deduction" | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/abusive-trust-tax-evasion-schemes-questions-and-answers (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: IRS, Abusive Trust Tax Evasion Schemes: Questions and Answers | "the trust is disregarded as a separate tax entity, and all income is taxed to the grantor." | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/abusive-trust-tax-evasion-schemes-questions-and-answers (read 2026-11-26)
