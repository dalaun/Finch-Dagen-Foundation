# Q38. Without moving, what can an Illinois founder lawfully get from a Delaware or Wyoming trust, and what can't he?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice. SYNTHETIC: no real figures. Each route is graded on two questions: does it change the Family Trust's Illinois income tax, and does it buy protection or control?

- Question: Illinois residents do hold Delaware and Wyoming trusts without moving. Which structures do they use, which rule does each rely on, and which ones change Illinois income tax rather than only protection, duration and control?
- Packet: A
- Coverage flag: none
- Confidence: open
- Assumes: the founder is the grantor, stays domiciled in Illinois, serves as a trustee or advisor, and family members in Illinois are beneficiaries (the rehearsal's facts; see Q22).
- Lawyer decides: Which route, if any, the Family Trust uses, knowing the grades below: routes 1, 4 and 5 change no Illinois income tax for this founder; route 2 changes it only if every Illinois contact is removed, which his own trusteeship rules out; route 3 (an incomplete-gift non-grantor trust) gains nothing in Illinois because the trust becomes an Illinois resident trust the day it is non-grantor. Counsel also decides whether a Delaware qualified trustee's fee is worth the protection and duration it buys, confirms the open points in claim 9, and confirms who the grantor is under claim 10.
- Claims:
  1. Stakes: Illinois taxes a trust's net income at 4.95 percent, plus a 1.5 percent replacement tax, about 6.45 percent in all. That is the most any route below could save on income Illinois may tax.
     - Trace: Illinois Department of Revenue, Income Tax Rates | "Trusts and estates – 4.95 percent of net income" | https://tax.illinois.gov/research/taxrates/income.html (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: Illinois Department of Revenue, Income Tax Rates | "Partnerships, trusts, and S corporations" ... "1.5 percent of net income" | https://tax.illinois.gov/research/taxrates/income.html (read 2026-11-26)
  2. Route 1, an out-of-state grantor trust (a Delaware asset protection trust or a Wyoming or Delaware dynasty trust). While it is a grantor trust its income is taxed to the founder, an Illinois resident, and the trust files no Illinois fiduciary return. Grade: no Illinois tax change. What it buys is protection, duration and directed-trust tools (claims 3 and 4).
     - Trace: 86 Ill. Adm. Code 100.3020(a)(4) | "a trust is considered irrevocable to the extent that the grantor is not treated as the owner" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-86-SS-100.3020 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: Illinois Department of Revenue, Fiduciary (Trust and Estate) | "trusts are not required to file Form IL-1041." | https://tax.illinois.gov/research/taxinformation/income/fiduciary.html (read 2026-11-26)
  3. What makes a trust a Delaware trust is a Delaware qualified trustee who does real work there, not an address. The trustee must be a Delaware-resident individual other than the founder, or a Delaware-authorized trust company, and must keep custody of some of the property or records in Delaware or otherwise take part in running it; the instrument must say Delaware law governs.
     - Trace: 12 Del. C. 3570(8)a | "In the case of a natural person, is a resident of this State other than the transferor" | https://delcode.delaware.gov/title12/c035/sc06/index.html (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 12 Del. C. 3570(8)b | "Maintains or arranges for custody in this State of some or all of the property" ... "or otherwise materially participates in the administration of the trust." | https://delcode.delaware.gov/title12/c035/sc06/index.html (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 12 Del. C. 3570(11)a | "Expressly incorporates the law of this State to govern the validity, construction and administration of the trust" | https://delcode.delaware.gov/title12/c035/sc06/index.html (read 2026-11-26)
  4. Route 4, his role. Delaware lets the founder stay on as the trust's investment adviser, though not as its trustee; Illinois treats any directing advisor as a fiduciary (Q8). Grade: keeps control of investments; no Illinois tax change. Under Linn (Q22 claim 9) an Illinois-resident advisor or protector is an Illinois contact, so this role also closes route 2.
     - Trace: 12 Del. C. 3570(8)d | "A person may serve as an investment adviser described in § 3313 of this title" ... "such a person may not serve as trustee or otherwise serve as adviser of a trust" | https://delcode.delaware.gov/title12/c035/sc06/index.html (read 2026-11-26)
  5. Duration: Delaware has no perpetuities limit for personal property held in trust (110 years for real property). Wyoming's 1,000-year period is pending, needs Dalaun (Q22). Grade: duration, not tax.
     - Trace: 25 Del. C. 503(b) | "the rule against perpetuities for real property held in trust is that at the expiration of 110 years" | https://delcode.delaware.gov/title25/c005/index.html (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 25 Del. C. 503(a) | "no interest created in personal property held in trust shall be void by reason of any rule" | https://delcode.delaware.gov/title25/c005/index.html (read 2026-11-26)
  6. Route 2, a non-grantor trust with too few Illinois contacts. By statute it is still an Illinois resident trust if the founder is domiciled in Illinois the day it becomes irrevocable for this test (Q22 claims 17 and 18). Only Linn's due process limit can take it out, on facts where the trustee, protector, beneficiaries, property and administration are all outside Illinois (Q22 claim 9, paragraph 33 pending). Grade: changes Illinois tax only if every contact leaves; the founder as Illinois trustee and Illinois beneficiaries each defeat it, and Illinois-source income such as an Illinois rental stays taxable anyway.
     - Trace: 35 ILCS 5/1501(a)(20)(D) | "the grantor of which was domiciled in this State at the time such trust became irrevocable" | https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/003500050K1501.htm (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
  7. Route 3, an incomplete-gift non-grantor trust (ING; Delaware's DING, Wyoming's WING, Nevada's NING). The founder keeps a power that makes the gift incomplete, and distributions need the consent of a committee of beneficiaries, who are adverse parties, so the trust is not a grantor trust. Grade in Illinois: no gain. Other states write ING statutes because they tax by where a trust is administered; Illinois taxes by the grantor's domicile, so the day the ING becomes non-grantor it is an Illinois resident trust under claim 6. New York (2014) and California (2023) went further and tax INGs as grantor trusts.
     - Trace: 26 CFR 25.2511-2(c) | "A gift is also incomplete if and to the extent that a reserved power gives the donor the power to name new beneficiaries" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/25.2511-2 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 25.2511-2(e) | "A donor is considered as himself having a power if it is exercisable by him in conjunction with any person" ... "not having a substantial adverse interest in the disposition of the transferred property" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/25.2511-2 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: IRC 672(a) | "the term “adverse party” means any person having a substantial beneficial interest" ... "in the trust which would be adversely affected by the exercise or nonexercise of the power which he possesses" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/672 (read 2026-11-26)
  8. Route 5, a P.O. box, a registered agent or a shell company in Delaware or Wyoming. None of them moves the founder's domicile, and his domicile is what Illinois tests. Domicile is the true, fixed, permanent home; a person has only one; an Illinois domicile is lost only by locating elsewhere with intent to stay and abandoning any intent to return. The evidence Illinois weighs is where family lives, voter and car registration, and the like; a mailing address is not on the list. A shell company owned by the trust changes who holds title, not where the trust resides. Grade: changes nothing, and claiming otherwise on a return is the red flag this check exists to catch.
     - Trace: 86 Ill. Adm. Code 100.3020(d) | "Domicile has been defined as the place where an individual has his or her true, fixed, permanent home" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-86-SS-100.3020 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 86 Ill. Adm. Code 100.3020(d) | "An individual can at any one time have but one domicile" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-86-SS-100.3020 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 86 Ill. Adm. Code 100.3020(d) | "by locating elsewhere with the intention of establishing the new location as his or her domicile; and" ... "by abandoning any intention of returning to Illinois." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-86-SS-100.3020 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 86 Ill. Adm. Code 100.3020(g)(1) | "location of spouse and dependents; voter registration; automobile registration or driver's license;" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-86-SS-100.3020 (read 2026-11-26)
  9. Open for counsel: no Illinois statute, regulation or ruling on INGs was found; whether the IRS still issues private letter rulings on ING gift and grantor status was not checked; two 2025 Illinois bills (SB0140, HB2368) were not read.

  10. Who the grantor is. For federal tax, a grantor is anyone who creates the trust or makes a gratuitous transfer to it (any transfer not for fair market value), so the person whose property funds the trust is its grantor whatever name the instrument carries. A person named as creator who funds nothing is not an owner, and one whose nominal funding is reimbursed is not treated as transferring anything. If one person creates or funds a trust on behalf of another, both are grantors. Grade: putting another person's name on the trust does not move the residence test off the founder if his property funds it. Whether Illinois reads "grantor" in 1501(a)(20)(D) the same way was not read; that is for counsel.
     - Trace: 26 CFR 1.671-2(e)(1) | "a grantor includes any person to the extent such person either creates a trust" ... "or directly or indirectly makes a gratuitous transfer" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.671-2 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 1.671-2(e)(2)(i) | "A gratuitous transfer is any transfer other than a transfer for fair market value" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.671-2 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 1.671-2(e)(1) | "a person who creates a trust but makes no gratuitous transfers to the trust is not treated as an owner" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.671-2 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 1.671-2(e)(1) | "If a person creates or funds a trust on behalf of another person, both persons are treated as grantors" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.671-2 (read 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 1.671-2(e)(1) and (e)(6), Example 3 | "a person who funds a trust with an amount that is directly reimbursed to such person" ... "A is not treated as transferring any property to FT" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.671-2 (read 2026-11-26)
