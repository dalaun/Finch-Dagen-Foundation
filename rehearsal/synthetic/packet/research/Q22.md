# Q22. Which state's law should govern the Family Trust, and where should it be administered?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: Should the Family Trust be governed by and administered under Illinois, Wyoming or Delaware law, comparing directed-trust statutes, state income tax on a non-grantor trust, and the situs and in-state trustee rules, and what does Illinois still control whatever the trust's state?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: open
- Assumes: Illinois situs for the Family Trust is the rehearsal's working assumption (the research so far); this question tests it.
- Lawyer decides: Which state governs and where the trust is administered. On the residence test (claims 17 to 19, with Linn in claim 9): today the founder lands in the Illinois branch, because he is domiciled in Illinois, so a Family Trust that becomes non-grantor while he lives here is an Illinois resident trust whatever state it names. Landing in the other branch would take his domicile being outside Illinois on the day the trust stops being a grantor trust, or, under Linn (holding still to be quoted), a trust with too few Illinois contacts. Even then, Illinois-source income (an Illinois rental) stays taxable in Illinois. Linn is the only route found out of residence, and an Illinois-resident trustee, an Illinois-resident advisor directing investments, Illinois real estate or Illinois beneficiaries are each a contact Illinois may rely on. Counsel decides whether any arrangement leaves too few Illinois contacts to tax, and whether Wyoming's directed-trust and spendthrift rules are worth a Wyoming qualified trustee's fee when they buy protection and control, not a lower state tax.

  17. Illinois residence test, branch by branch (Dalaun's check, 2026-11-26: a trust named under another state's law can become an Illinois trust, and there is a way it does not). Illinois residence turns on the grantor's or decedent's domicile at one moment, not on the state the instrument names. A trust made by will is resident if the decedent died domiciled in Illinois; a living trust is resident if its grantor was domiciled in Illinois when it became irrevocable, and it counts as irrevocable only for the years the grantor is not taxed as its owner. Anyone the definition does not reach is a nonresident.
