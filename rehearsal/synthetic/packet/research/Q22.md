# Q22. Which state's law should govern the Family Trust, and where should it be administered?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: Should the Family Trust be governed by and administered under Illinois, Wyoming or Delaware law, comparing directed-trust statutes, state income tax on a non-grantor trust, and the situs and in-state trustee rules, and what does Illinois still control whatever the trust's state?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: open
- Assumes: Illinois situs for the Family Trust is the rehearsal's working assumption (the research so far); this question tests it.
- Lawyer decides: Which state governs and where the trust is administered. Today the founder lands in the Illinois branch of the residence test (claims 17 to 19): he is domiciled in Illinois, so a Family Trust that stops being a grantor trust while he lives here is an Illinois resident trust whatever state it names. The other branch would take his domicile outside Illinois on that day, or too few Illinois contacts under Linn (claim 9), which an Illinois trustee, advisor, real estate or beneficiaries each work against; Illinois-source income stays taxable either way. Counsel also decides whether Wyoming's directed-trust and spendthrift rules are worth a Wyoming qualified trustee's fee when they buy protection and control, not a lower state tax.

  6. Illinois: an irrevocable trust is an Illinois resident trust when its grantor was domiciled in Illinois at the time it became irrevocable, and it counts as irrevocable only to the extent the grantor is not treated as its owner under IRC 671 to 678. So a non-grantor Family Trust he creates while domiciled in Illinois is, in the statute's words, an Illinois resident trust whatever state its instrument names; a grantor trust is not, because its income is his. A trustee is a fiduciary under the Act.
     - Trace: 35 ILCS 5/1501(a)(20)(D) | "an irrevocable trust, the grantor of which was domiciled in this State at the time such trust became irrevocable" | https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/003500050K1501.htm (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 35 ILCS 5/1501(a)(20)(D) | "a trust shall be considered irrevocable to the extent that the grantor is not treated as the owner thereof under Sections 671 through 678 of the Internal Revenue Code" | https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/003500050K1501.htm (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 35 ILCS 5/1501(a)(7) | "The term "fiduciary" means a guardian, trustee, executor, administrator, receiver, or any person acting in any fiduciary capacity for any person." | https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/003500050K1501.htm (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
  7. Illinois has Delaware's directed-trust tools: a directed trustee who follows an advisor is liable only for willful misconduct, and a trust protector may move situs or governing law, with 60 days' notice to the Attorney General when the trust holds a charitable interest. None of this moves the trust's Illinois residence for income tax (claim 6).
     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/808(d)(6) | "change the situs of the trust, the governing law of the trust, or both" | https://ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/076000030K808.htm (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)

     - Trace: 760 ILCS 3/808(f)(1) | "except in cases of willful misconduct on the part of the excluded fiduciary in complying with the direction of the directing party" | https://ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/076000030K808.htm (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
  8. Wyoming: a trust's choice of Wyoming as its principal place of administration holds if a trustee lives or does business in Wyoming, if any part of the administration happens there, or if the settlor lived there when the trust was made; Wyoming law then governs its administration. An Illinois-resident trustee with no Wyoming administration meets none of the three.
     - Trace: W.S. 4-10-108(a) | "terms of a trust designating the principal place of administration are valid and controlling if" | https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title04.pdf (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: W.S. 4-10-108(a)(i) | "A trustee's principal place of business is located in, or a trustee is a resident of, the designated jurisdiction" | https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title04.pdf (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: W.S. 4-10-108(a)(ii) | "All or part of the administration occurs in the designated jurisdiction" | https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title04.pdf (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: W.S. 4-10-108(f)(i) | "The laws of this state shall govern the administration of any trust" ... "For which the principal place of administration of the trust is Wyoming" | https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title04.pdf (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)

  11. Wyoming directed trusts: a trust advisor may direct distributions and is a fiduciary to that extent, and a trustee who follows directions is not liable for the advisor's acts unless the instrument says otherwise.
     - Trace: W.S. 4-10-712(a)(v) | "To direct, consent or disapprove a trustee's or cotrustee's action or inaction in making distributions to beneficiaries" | https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title04.pdf (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)

  12. Wyoming trust protectors hold the powers the instrument gives them, which may include amending the instrument for tax status or changes in law, and are fiduciaries to that extent.
     - Trace: W.S. 4-10-710(a)(i) | "To modify or amend the trust instrument to achieve favorable tax status" | https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title04.pdf (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)

  17. Illinois residence test (Dalaun's check, 2026-11-26: a trust named under another state's law can become an Illinois trust, and there is a way it does not). Residence turns on the grantor's or decedent's domicile at one moment, not on the state the instrument names: a trust made by will is resident if the decedent died domiciled in Illinois, a living trust if its grantor was domiciled in Illinois when it became irrevocable. Anyone the definition does not reach is a nonresident.
     - Trace: 35 ILCS 5/1501(a)(20)(C) | "a trust created by a will of a decedent who at his death was domiciled in this State" | https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/003500050K1501.htm (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 35 ILCS 5/1501(a)(20)(D) | "an irrevocable trust, the grantor of which was domiciled in this State at the time such trust became irrevocable" | https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/003500050K1501.htm (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
     - Trace: 35 ILCS 5/1501(a)(14) | "The term "nonresident" means a person who is not a resident." | https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/003500050K1501.htm (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)

A grantor trust files no Illinois fiduciary return. No Illinois regulation or case on the timing has been read; counsel confirms it.
     - Trace: 35 ILCS 5/1501(a)(20)(D) | "a trust shall be considered irrevocable to the extent that the grantor is not treated as the owner thereof under Sections 671 through 678 of the Internal Revenue Code" | https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/003500050K1501.htm (copied by Dalaun 2026-11-26)
