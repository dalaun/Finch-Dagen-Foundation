# Q22. Which state's law should govern the Family Trust, and where should it be administered?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: Should the Family Trust be governed by and administered under Illinois, Wyoming or Delaware law, comparing directed-trust statutes, state income tax on a non-grantor trust, and the situs and in-state trustee rules, and what does Illinois still control whatever the trust's state?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: open
- Assumes: Illinois situs for the Family Trust is the rehearsal's working assumption (the research so far); this question tests it.
- Lawyer decides: Which state governs and where the trust is administered, whether the founder's Illinois residence makes the trust an Illinois resident trust whatever state it is sited in, and what an in-state trustee or agent is required.
- Claims:
  1. Delaware: a person given authority to direct a fiduciary's distribution decisions is an adviser and a fiduciary when exercising it, unless the instrument says otherwise.
     - Trace: 12 Del. C. 3313(a) | "such persons shall be considered to be advisers and fiduciaries when exercising such authority" | https://delcode.delaware.gov/title12/c033/index.html (read 2026-11-25)
  2. Delaware: a trustee that follows an adviser's direction is not liable for the resulting loss, except for wilful misconduct.
     - Trace: 12 Del. C. 3313(b) | "the fiduciary shall not be liable for any loss resulting directly or indirectly from any such act" | https://delcode.delaware.gov/title12/c033/index.html (read 2026-11-25)
  3. Delaware: an adviser who accepts appointment submits to personal jurisdiction of Delaware, which bears on the in-state reach question.
     - Trace: 12 Del. C. 3313(g) | "submits to personal jurisdiction of this State regarding any matter related to the trust" | https://delcode.delaware.gov/title12/c033/index.html (read 2026-11-25)
  4. Delaware income tax applies to trusts, and a resident trust gets a deduction only for income set aside for future distribution to nonresident beneficiaries; the model pays income currently to Illinois-resident beneficiaries, so this deduction is not shown to apply (counsel and CPA to confirm).
     - Trace: 30 Del. C. 1631 | "shall apply to the taxable income of estates and trusts" | https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c016/sc03/index.html (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: 30 Del. C. 1636(a) | "set aside for future distribution to nonresident beneficiaries" | https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c016/sc03/index.html (read 2026-11-25)
  5. Illinois: the Department of Revenue says a trust files an Illinois return if it is a resident of Illinois (and that grantor trusts do not file Form IL-1041); the page does not say what makes a trust an Illinois resident.
     - Trace: Illinois Department of Revenue, Fiduciary Income Tax | "is a resident of Illinois, is required to file, or files a federal income tax return" | https://tax.illinois.gov/research/taxinformation/income/fiduciary.html (read 2026-11-25)
  6. Illinois: whether a trust with an Illinois-resident grantor is an Illinois resident trust (35 ILCS 5/1501(a)(20)), and what an Illinois-resident trustee changes.
     - Trace: pending, needs Dalaun | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=251&ChapterID=7 (tried 2026-11-25)
  7. Illinois directed-trust and divided-trusteeship provisions (760 ILCS 3, Articles 8 and 12), for comparison with Delaware above.
     - Trace: pending, needs Dalaun | https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/details?ActID=4001&ActName=Illinois+Trust+Code.&ChapAct=760+ILCS+3%2F (tried 2026-11-25)
  8. Wyoming: directed trust and trust advisor provisions (Title 4) and state income tax on a trust. Wyoming's statutes were reachable only as a PDF (https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title04.pdf), which this research does not quote; no HTML page was found.
     - Trace: pending, needs Dalaun | https://wyoleg.gov/Legislation/StateStatutes (tried 2026-11-25)
  9. What Illinois still controls whatever the trust's state: a deed to Illinois real estate under Illinois property law, and the Illinois courts' reach over an Illinois-resident trustee.
