# Q15. How is a 10 percent set-aside to the foundation taxed, and does it make the trust a split-interest trust?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: If the Family Trust pays 10 percent of its distribution to the foundation as a set-aside deduction under 642(c)(1), how is that taxed to the trust and the beneficiaries, and does it make the trust a 4947(a)(2) split-interest trust? (See Q9 for the self-dealing side.)
- Packet: B
- Confidence: likely
- Lawyer decides: Whether the instrument's wording meets 642(c)(1) (paid under the governing instrument, from gross income), and whether the trust is split-interest.
- Claims:
  1. The trust may deduct gross income paid for a 170(c) purpose under the governing instrument, without a percentage limit.
     - Trace: IRC 642(c)(1) | "any amount of the gross income, without limitation, which pursuant to the terms of the governing instrument is, during the taxable year, paid for a purpose specified in section 170(c)" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/642 (read 2026-11-25)
  2. Distributable net income is computed without the 642(c) deduction when income required to be distributed exceeds it.
     - Trace: IRC 662(a)(1) | "(computed without the deduction allowed by section 642(c), relating to deduction for charitable, etc., purposes)" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/662 (read 2026-11-25)
  3. A trust with a 642(c) deduction can be a split-interest trust under 4947(a)(2).
     - Trace: IRC 4947(a)(2) | "which has amounts in trust for which a deduction was allowed under section 170, 545(b)(2), 642(c), 2055, 2106(a)(2), or 2522" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4947 (read 2026-11-25)
