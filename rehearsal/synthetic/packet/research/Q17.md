# Q17. How big must the asset be to fund a 10 percent Foundation share and still beat doing nothing?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: If 10 percent of the Family Trust's distribution goes to the foundation (see Q15), what yearly net asset income, and so what asset size, makes the structure pay for itself, and is the share worth that size?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: open
- Lawyer decides: Whether the 10 percent share is worth its cost; the size itself is an engine output and a yield assumption, which no authority read here gives.
- Claims:
  1. The set-aside is deductible by the trust only if paid under the governing instrument for a 170(c) purpose, so the 10 percent share depends on the instrument's wording (see Q15).
     - Trace: IRC 642(c)(1) | "any amount of the gross income, without limitation, which pursuant to the terms of the governing instrument is, during the taxable year, paid for a purpose specified in section 170(c)" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/642 (read 2026-11-25)
  2. A trust that has amounts in trust for which a 642(c) deduction was allowed can be a split-interest trust.
     - Trace: IRC 4947(a)(2) | "which has amounts in trust for which a deduction was allowed under section 170, 545(b)(2), 642(c), 2055, 2106(a)(2), or 2522" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4947 (read 2026-11-25)
  3. A yield of 0.07 turns a yearly net income of 350,000 into an asset of about 5,000,000, and the 350,000 break-even is the engine's first crossing.
     - Trace: no authority found | searched: law.cornell.edu uscode 26 sections 642 and 4947 (give tax rules, no yields or break-even sizes) (read 2026-11-25)
