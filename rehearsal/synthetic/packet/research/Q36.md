# Q36. How much will the management company really pay the founder in consulting revenue each year?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice. SYNTHETIC: 24,000 is a scenario input, not a real figure.

- Question: What yearly amount can the management company lawfully pay the founder for services actually done, and how is it supported?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: open
- Lawyer decides: Whether the pay is reasonable for the services actually done, and how it is documented; the amount is a fact only a CPA and the founder can supply.
- Claims:
  1. A deduction for pay is allowed only for a reasonable amount for services actually rendered.
     - Trace: 26 CFR 1.162-7(a) | "a reasonable allowance for salaries or other compensation for personal services actually rendered" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.162-7 (read 2026-11-25)
  2. Reasonable means what like enterprises would pay under like circumstances, judged when the contract was made.
     - Trace: 26 CFR 1.162-7(b)(3) | "such amount as would ordinarily be paid for like services by like enterprises under like circumstances" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.162-7 (read 2026-11-25)
  3. The pay is gross income to the founder as compensation for services.
     - Trace: IRC 61(a)(1) | "Compensation for services, including fees, commissions, fringe benefits, and similar items" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/61 (read 2026-11-25)
  4. If the management company is an S corporation, a 2-percent shareholder is treated as a partner for fringe benefits, which affects how pay and fringe benefits are reported.
     - Trace: IRC 1372(a) | "any 2-percent shareholder of the S corporation shall be treated as a partner of such partnership" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1372 (read 2026-11-25)
  5. If the Foundation were the payer, the pay would have to be reasonable and necessary to be allowed.
     - Trace: IRC 4941(d)(2)(E) | "personal services which are reasonable and necessary to carrying out the exempt purpose of the private foundation shall not be an act of self-dealing if the compensation (or payment or reimbursement) is not excessive" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4941 (read 2026-11-25)
  6. The amount (24,000, 12,000 or 48,000 a year) the management company can pay.
     - Trace: no authority found | searched: IRC 61, 162, 1372; Treas. Reg. 1.162-7 (read 2026-11-25); the amount is a business fact, benchmarked only by a pay survey for like work, none of which was read
