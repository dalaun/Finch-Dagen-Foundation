# Q35. Can the Foundation meet its payout through grants to other foundations or collaboratives?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice. SYNTHETIC: no real grantees or names.

- Question: When does a grant from the Foundation to another private foundation or a collaborative count toward its yearly payout, what must the Foundation do to make the grant lawful, and when would an exchange of grants between foundations be self-dealing or a step transaction?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: open
- Lawyer decides: Whether the Foundation grants only to public charities, or also to other foundations (and under what controls), and who writes the grant agreements and reports.
- Claims:
  1. A grant to another private foundation that is not an operating foundation, or to an organization the Foundation or its disqualified persons control, is not a qualifying distribution by itself.
     - Trace: IRC 4942(g)(1)(A) | "other than any contribution to (i) an organization controlled (directly or indirectly) by the foundation or one or more disqualified persons" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4942 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4942(g)(1)(A)(ii) | "a private foundation which is not an operating foundation" | same address (read 2026-11-25)
  2. The exception, the redistribution rule: such a grant counts if the receiving organization pays out an equal amount as a qualifying distribution by the end of its next taxable year, treated as out of corpus, and the giving foundation keeps adequate records showing it.
     - Trace: IRC 4942(g)(3)(A) | "not later than the close of the first taxable year after its taxable year in which such contribution is received, such organization makes a distribution equal to the amount of such contribution" | same address (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4942(g)(3)(B) | "obtains adequate records or other sufficient evidence from such organization showing that the qualifying distribution described in subparagraph (A) has been made by such organization." | same address (read 2026-11-25)
  3. A grant to an organization is a taxable expenditure unless the grantee is a public charity (509(a)(1) or (2)), certain 509(a)(3) supporting organizations, or an exempt operating foundation, or the Foundation exercises expenditure responsibility.
     - Trace: IRC 4945(d)(4) | "as a grant to an organization unless" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4945 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4945(d)(4)(B) | "the private foundation exercises expenditure responsibility with respect to such grant in accordance with subsection (h)" | same address (read 2026-11-25)
  4. Expenditure responsibility means three duties: see that the grant is spent only for its purpose, obtain full and complete reports from the grantee, and report on the expenditures to the Secretary.
     - Trace: IRC 4945(h) | "to see that the grant is spent solely for the purpose for which made, (2) to obtain full and complete reports from the grantee on how the funds are spent, and (3) to make full and detailed reports with respect to such expenditures to the Secretary." | same address (read 2026-11-25)
  5. Self-dealing covers any direct or indirect transfer to, or use for the benefit of, a disqualified person of the Foundation's income or assets, so an exchange of grants arranged to benefit a disqualified person is tested under 4941 and not only by the form of each grant.
     - Trace: IRC 4941(d)(1) | "any direct or indirect—" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4941 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4941(d)(1)(E) | "transfer to, or use by or for the benefit of, a disqualified person of the income or assets of a private foundation" | same address (read 2026-11-25)
- Not traced: Treasury regulations on expenditure responsibility (26 CFR 53.4945-5) and on indirect self-dealing (26 CFR 53.4941(d)-1); the 4942(g)(4) limit on grants to supporting organizations; whether a pooled fund or "collaborative" is itself a private foundation, a public charity or a donor-advised fund (a facts question); the step-transaction doctrine, which is case law and was not read.

- Route in one line: a grant to a public charity is the plain route; a grant to another private foundation can count toward payout only if the grantee re-pays an equal amount out of corpus by the end of its next year with records on file, and needs expenditure responsibility besides; any prearranged exchange of grants that benefits a disqualified person is tested as self-dealing, so no route is modeled and the engine counts only the Foundation's own program output. Route status: a lawful route exists on the text read, with controls; it is not modeled.
