# Q19. Can any operating-company or business-trust equity go to the Foundation, and how much?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: If the founder or the Family Trust wants the Foundation to hold equity in an operating company or a business trust, what does IRC 4943 allow, and who counts as a disqualified person whose holdings are added to the Foundation's?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: likely
- Lawyer decides: Whether any business equity goes to the Foundation at all, how much, whether the Foundation's holdings combined with the family's stay inside the limit, and how the five-year disposal period is managed if they do not.
- Claims:
  1. The tax is 10 percent of the value of excess business holdings for each taxable year in the taxable period.
     - Trace: IRC 4943(a)(1) | "a tax equal to 10 percent of the value of such holdings" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
  2. If excess holdings remain at the close of the taxable period, a second tax of 200 percent applies.
     - Trace: IRC 4943(b) | "there is hereby imposed a tax equal to 200 percent of such excess business holdings" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
  3. In a corporation, the Foundation's permitted holdings are 20 percent of the voting stock reduced by the percentage held by all disqualified persons, so family holdings and the Foundation's share one limit.
     - Trace: IRC 4943(c)(2)(A) | "20 percent of the voting stock, reduced by (ii) the percentage of the voting stock owned by all disqualified persons" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
  4. The limit is 35 percent where a person who is not a disqualified person has effective control, shown to the Secretary's satisfaction.
     - Trace: IRC 4943(c)(2)(B) | "subparagraph (A) shall be applied by substituting 35 percent for 20 percent" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
  5. No excess holdings in a corporation where the Foundation, with related private foundations, owns not more than 2 percent of the voting stock (and of the value of all classes).
     - Trace: IRC 4943(c)(2)(C) | "shall not be treated as having excess business holdings in any corporation in which it" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
  6. Holdings received by gift or bequest are treated as held by a disqualified person for 5 years, which is the disposal window.
     - Trace: IRC 4943(c)(6) | "shall (while held by the foundation) be treated as held by a disqualified person (rather than by the foundation) during the 5-year period beginning on the date of such change in holdings" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
  7. The Secretary may extend that period by another 5 years for certain large gifts and bequests.
     - Trace: IRC 4943(c)(7) | "The Secretary may extend for an additional 5-year period the period under paragraph (6)" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
  8. A business enterprise does not include a functionally related business or one with at least 95 percent passive gross income, so a passive holding is outside the tax.
     - Trace: IRC 4943(d)(3) | "a trade or business at least 95 percent of the gross income of which is derived from passive sources" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
  9. A route exists for 100 percent foundation ownership: the foundation holds all the voting stock, acquired other than by purchase, and the enterprise distributes its net operating income to the foundation within 120 days after year end (independently operated; paragraph (4) not quoted here).
     - Trace: IRC 4943(g)(2)-(3) | "100 percent of the voting stock in the business enterprise is held by the private foundation at all times during the taxable year" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
  10. Disqualified persons include a substantial contributor, a foundation manager, a family member of either, and a corporation, partnership or trust in which they hold more than 35 percent.
     - Trace: IRC 4946(a)(1)(E) | "a corporation of which persons described in subparagraph (A), (B), (C), or (D) own more than 35 percent of the total combined voting power" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4946 (read 2026-11-25)
  11. A private foundation controlled by the same persons is a disqualified person for 4943 only.
     - Trace: IRC 4946(a)(1)(H) | "only for purposes of section 4943, a private foundation" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4946 (read 2026-11-25)
  12. For a partnership or joint venture, permitted holdings are set by regulation in the same principle as the corporate rule, with "profits interest" in place of "voting stock" and "capital interest" in place of "nonvoting stock"; a sole proprietorship has no permitted holdings; in any other case (a business trust, for example) "beneficial interest" replaces "voting stock".
     - Trace: IRC 4943(c)(3) | "The permitted holdings of a private foundation in any business enterprise which is not incorporated shall be determined under regulations prescribed by the Secretary." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4943(c)(3)(A) | "in the case of a partnership or joint venture, "profits interest" shall be substituted for "voting stock"," | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4943(c)(3)(B) | "in the case of a proprietorship, there shall be no permitted holdings" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4943(c)(3)(C) | "in any other case, "beneficial interest" shall be substituted for "voting stock"." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4943 (read 2026-11-25)
  13. The regulation applies the paragraph (b) principles to unincorporated enterprises: profits interest for partnerships, and beneficial interest for trusts and other enterprises, which includes a right to a share of profit distributions.
     - Trace: 26 CFR 53.4943-3(c)(1) | "The permitted holdings of a private foundation in any business enterprise which is not incorporated" ... "be determined under the principles of paragraph (b) of this section." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/53.4943-3 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 53.4943-3(c)(2) | "the terms “profits interest” and “capital interest” shall be substituted for “voting stock”" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/53.4943-3 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 53.4943-3(c)(4) | "the term “beneficial interest” shall be substituted for “voting stock”" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/53.4943-3 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: 26 CFR 53.4943-3(c)(4) | "includes any right to receive a portion of distributions of profits of such enterprise" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/53.4943-3 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Reading (not a quote): the 20 percent less disqualified-person percent test therefore runs on profits interest for a partnership and on beneficial interest for the business trust; the page summaries read in this run also say a trust's beneficial interest is its remainder interest under 53.4943-8(b) and that profits interest follows the 704(b) distributive share (not quoted; 53.4943-8(b) not read).
- Not traced: 26 CFR 53.4943-3(c)(2) measurement of capital interest and 53.4943-8(b) (remainder interest of a trust), not quoted in this run.

- Route in one line: with the founder's family holding most of a company, the Foundation's permitted share is near zero (20 percent less family percent), so a gift of operating equity has no route unless the family holds under 20 percent combined, a third party controls (35 percent), the interest is passive, or it is a 100 percent independently operated holding; the same arithmetic runs on profits interest in a partnership and beneficial interest in a business trust, and a sole proprietorship has no permitted holding.
