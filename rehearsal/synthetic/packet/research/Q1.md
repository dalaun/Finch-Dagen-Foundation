# Q1. Do the rentals, and a bought business run by a hired manager, count as passive?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: Would my rental income, and a business I buy and run through a hired manager, be passive activities for me under section 469, and could I qualify as a real estate professional?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: likely
- Lawyer decides: Whether his own facts (hours, who runs the business) meet material participation or the real estate professional test; the CPA decides, the statute's tests are traced below.
- Claims:
  1. Rental activity is passive by default, except for a real estate professional.
     - Trace: IRC 469(c)(2) | "Passive activity includes any rental activity" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/469 (read 2026-11-25)
  2. A business is not passive only if the taxpayer materially participates, meaning regular, continuous and substantial involvement in its operations.
     - Trace: IRC 469(h)(1) | "involved in the operations of the activity on a basis which is" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/469 (read 2026-11-25)
  3. The real estate professional exception needs over half his personal services in real property businesses and more than 750 hours.
     - Trace: IRC 469(c)(7)(B) | "more than one-half of the personal services performed in trades or businesses by the taxpayer" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/469 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 469(c)(7)(B) | "more than 750 hours of services during the taxable year in real property trades or businesses" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/469 (read 2026-11-25)
  4. The 3.8 percent net investment income tax is the rate the engine applies to the passive income.
     - Trace: IRC 1411(a)(1) | "a tax equal to 3.8 percent of the lesser of" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1411 (read 2026-11-25)
