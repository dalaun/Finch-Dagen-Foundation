# Q7. Do the powers he keeps cause gift or estate tax problems for the funder?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: Does my holding a principal power under a health, education, support or maintenance standard cause estate or gift tax problems for me as the funder (sections not modeled here)?
- Packet: A
- Coverage flag: none
- Confidence: open
- Lawyer decides: Whether the powers the founder keeps as trustee (route R2 or R3) pull trust property into his estate or make his transfers incomplete gifts; the standard-limited power cases were not found in the pages read.
- Claims:
  1. Property is in the gross estate when the founder kept the right, alone or with another person, to say who enjoys the property or its income.
     - Trace: IRC 2036(a)(2) | "to designate the persons who shall possess or enjoy the property or the income therefrom" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/2036 (read 2026-11-25)
  2. Property is also in the gross estate when a power he holds can change who enjoys it at his death.
     - Trace: IRC 2038(a)(1) | "by trust or otherwise, where the enjoyment thereof was subject at the date of his death to any change through the exercise of a power" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/2038 (read 2026-11-25)
  3. The gift tax reaches a transfer in trust as well as an outright one.
     - Trace: IRC 2511(a) | "whether the transfer is in trust or otherwise, whether the gift is direct or indirect" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/2511 (read 2026-11-25)
  4. Whether a power limited to a health, education, support or maintenance standard avoids 2036 and 2038 is not answered by the statute text read here.
     - Trace: no authority found | searched: 26 USC 2036 and 2038 on law.cornell.edu for an ascertainable-standard exception (read 2026-11-25)
  5. Reserved power to designate who receives the income puts the property in the estate under the regulation too.
     - Trace: Treas. Reg. 20.2036-1(b)(3) | "a reserved power to designate the person or persons to receive the income from the transferred property" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/20.2036-1 (read 2026-11-25)
  6. 26 CFR 20.2036-1 says nothing about an ascertainable-standard exception.
     - Trace: no authority found | searched: 26 CFR 20.2036-1 on law.cornell.edu for fixed or ascertainable standard (read 2026-11-25)
  7. A reserved power to name new beneficiaries makes the gift incomplete unless it is a fiduciary power limited by a fixed or ascertainable standard.
     - Trace: Treas. Reg. 25.2511-2(c) | "unless the power is a fiduciary power limited by a fixed or ascertainable standard" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/25.2511-2 (read 2026-11-25)
  8. A donor-trustee with no beneficial interest and only standard-limited fiduciary powers makes a completed gift.
     - Trace: Treas. Reg. 25.2511-2(g) | "the exercise or nonexercise of which is limited by a fixed or ascertainable standard" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/25.2511-2 (read 2026-11-25)
