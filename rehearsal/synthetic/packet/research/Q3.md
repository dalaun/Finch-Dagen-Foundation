# Q3. Keep the Family Trust non-grantor while he stays its steward

Research for counsel review, not legal advice.

- Question: Can the Family Trust be written so the founder is trustee for investments and administration while either an independent co-trustee makes distribution decisions or income is paid in fixed shares with principal limited to health, education, support and maintenance, and the trust stays non-grantor?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: likely
- Lawyer decides: Whether the drafted terms, including who counts as related or subordinate, actually fit the 674(c) or 674(b)(5)(A) exception.
- Claims:
  1. Section 674(a) makes the grantor the owner when a disposition power is held by the grantor or a nonadverse party without an adverse party's consent.
     - Trace: IRC 674(a) | "exercisable by the grantor or a nonadverse party, or both, without the approval or consent of any adverse party." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/674 (read 2026-11-25)
  2. The 674(c) exception covers a power held only by trustees, none of them the grantor, with no more than half related or subordinate.
     - Trace: IRC 674(c) | "by a trustee or trustees, none of whom is the grantor, and no more than half of whom are related or subordinate parties" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/674 (read 2026-11-25)
  3. A principal power limited by a reasonably definite standard in the instrument is excepted under 674(b)(5)(A).
     - Trace: IRC 674(b)(5)(A) | "provided that the power is limited by a reasonably definite standard which is set forth in the trust instrument; or" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/674 (read 2026-11-25)
  4. A grantor's spouse living with the grantor counts as a related or subordinate party.
     - Trace: IRC 672(c) | "the grantor’s spouse if living with the grantor;" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/672 (read 2026-11-25)
