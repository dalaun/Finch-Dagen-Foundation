# Q34. Can the founder be paid a one-dollar salary plus fringe benefits by the operating and management companies?

Research for counsel review, not legal advice. SYNTHETIC: no real pay or names.

- Question: If the founder takes a token salary from the operating and management companies and receives fringe benefits instead, when is that lawful, when does the IRS recharacterize it, and why can the Foundation or the split-interest trust never pay or provide any of it?
- Packet: B
- Confidence: open
- Lawyer decides: Whether the founder's pay is set at a market wage (the engine's assumption) or at a lower salary with listed fringes, and who writes the written support for it.
- Claims:
  1. An S corporation must pay a shareholder-employee reasonable compensation for services, and the IRS may reclassify non-wage distributions as wages.
     - Trace: IRS, S corporation compensation and medical insurance issues | "S corporations must pay reasonable compensation to a shareholder-employee in return for services" | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-compensation-and-medical-insurance-issues (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: same page | "The IRS has the authority to reclassify payments made to shareholders from non-wage distributions" | same address (read 2026-11-25)
  2. For fringe benefits, an S corporation is treated as a partnership and a more-than-2-percent shareholder as a partner, so the employee-fringe exclusions do not carry over as they would for an ordinary employee.
     - Trace: IRC 1372(a) | "the S corporation shall be treated as a partnership, and" ... "any 2-percent shareholder of the S corporation shall be treated as a partner of such partnership." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1372 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 1372(b) | "more than 2 percent of the outstanding stock of such corporation" | same address (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRS page above | "Health and accident insurance premiums paid on behalf of a greater than 2-percent S corporation shareholder-employee" | same IRS address (read 2026-11-25; the sentence was cut at the quote limit, the page's treatment of it was not read in full)
  3. The fringes that can be excluded from an employee's income are a closed list, and a working condition fringe is limited to what the employee could deduct under 162 or 167.
     - Trace: IRC 132(a) | "Gross income shall not include any fringe benefit which qualifies as a" ... "(3) working condition fringe," ... "(4) de minimis fringe," | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/132 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 132(d) | "such payment would be allowable as a deduction under section 162 or 167." | same address (read 2026-11-25)
  4. Employer-provided health coverage is excluded from an employee's income; meals and lodging are excluded only if furnished for the employer's convenience and on its business premises.
     - Trace: IRC 106(a) | "gross income of an employee does not include employer-provided coverage under an accident or health plan." | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/106 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 119(a) | "for the convenience of the employer, but only if" ... "the meals are furnished on the business premises of the employer" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/119 (read 2026-11-25)
  5. None of this may come from the Foundation or the split-interest trust: using its income or assets for a disqualified person is self-dealing, and the compensation exception covers only personal services reasonable and necessary to its exempt purpose.
     - Trace: IRC 4941(d)(1)(E) | "transfer to, or use by or for the benefit of, a disqualified person of the income or assets of a private foundation" | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4941 (read 2026-11-25)
     - Trace: IRC 4941(d)(2)(E) | "for personal services which are reasonable and necessary to carrying out the exempt purpose of the private foundation" | same address (read 2026-11-25)
- Not traced: C corporation pay versus dividends; section 105 and its more-than-2-percent limits; the amount that counts as reasonable pay (a facts question); whether the 4947(a)(2) trust is bound by 4941 as the packet assumes (Q9, Q15).

- Route in one line: a token salary is lawful only if it is still reasonable for the services actually done, which the IRS can test and reclassify; fringes help only inside the closed list and, for an S corporation owner above 2 percent, are treated as a partner's; nothing may be paid by the Foundation or the split-interest trust. The engine keeps the founder at a market wage.
