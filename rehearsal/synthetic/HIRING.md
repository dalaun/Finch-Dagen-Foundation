## The question

Three company benefits (education under 127, dependent care under 129, the cafeteria plan under 125) limit how much can go to owners. A company whose only employee is its owner fails all three. So the question is whether hiring makes them work, and whether hiring family counts.

The short answer from this run: hiring only to unlock a benefit never pays, and family is counted as the owner. Unrelated staff in real roles at market pay unlock 129 and 125 at six hires. They never unlock 127 at any realistic size. The tax value to the owner is a few thousand dollars a year. So a hire has to be justified by the work it does. The benefits are a small extra on top of a real company, never the reason for one.

## Four checks on every hire

Each hire has to pass all four. They are separate, and passing one never fixes another.

| Check | Test | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| HA1 Arm's length | Real work for the company, pay within 80% to 110% of market. Household work in the owner's home always fails | 162(a)(1) reasonable compensation; 262(a); Pub. 926; Topic 756; band ASSUMPTION, Q49 |
| HA2 Substance | A purpose for the hire, written before the start, other than unlocking a benefit | 7701(o)(1)(B); SUBSTANCE.md SB1 |
| HA3 Attribution | The hire isn't counted as the owner under 1563 (for 127 and 129) or 318 (for 125's key-employee test) | 1563(e)(5), (e)(6); 318(a)(1); 416(i)(1) |
| HA4 Board | Approved before the start by approvers with no stake who aren't the owner's family, with market comparables and minutes | declared-plan pattern; independence ASSUMPTION, Q48 |

Arm's length asks whether the deal is what strangers would agree to. Attribution asks who the law counts as the owner. A parent hired at exactly market pay passes arm's length and still fails attribution, because the law counts the parent as the owner. A stranger at twice market pay passes attribution and fails arm's length.

### What the board does and doesn't do

- **For Canon Management (the company):** approval before money moves, with comparables and minutes, is evidence that the pay is reasonable under 162.
- **For the Foundation:** the same record is evidence that pay to a disqualified person is reasonable under 4941(d)(2)(E). It is not a cure for self-dealing. A payment that 4941(d)(2)(E) doesn't cover stays self-dealing however the board votes.
- **It never changes attribution.** A perfect board record on a parent's hire still leaves the parent counted as the owner (self-test).
- The owner approving their own hire is not independent, and approval dated after the start fails (self-tests).

## What was read (2026-11-26)

- **127(b)(3):** no more than 5% of the amounts paid in a year may go to owners of more than 5% of the employer, or their spouses or dependents. Ownership under 127(c)(4)(A) follows 1563(d) and (e), without regard to (e)(3)(C). Source: law.cornell.edu.
- **129(d)(4):** no more than 25% to owners of more than 5%, with ownership under 129(e)(5)(A). **129(d)(8)(A):** the average benefit to non-highly-compensated employees must be at least 55% of the average to highly compensated ones. **129(d)(8)(B):** for salary reduction, employees paid under $25,000 may be left out. **129(c):** no exclusion for care paid to the employee's dependent or child under 19. **129(a)(2)(A):** cap $7,500 (Pub. L. 119-21).
- **125(b)(2):** key employees may get no more than 25% of the qualified benefits. Key employee per 416(i)(1): an officer over the adjusted threshold, a 5% owner, or a 1% owner over $150,000, with 318 applying.
- **1563(e)(5):** spouse. **(e)(6)(A):** children under 21. **(e)(6)(B):** if the individual owns over 50%, also parents, grandparents, grandchildren and adult children. No siblings.
- **318(a)(1):** spouse, children, grandchildren and parents. No siblings and no grandparents.
- **Topic 756 and Pub. 926 (2026):** maids, butlers and similar workers in a private home are household employees. The household employer owes Social Security and Medicare once one worker is paid $3,000 or more in 2026, and FUTA once more than $1,000 is paid in a quarter, on Schedule H. Pub. 926 says "services performed as a private secretary, tutor, or librarian, even though performed in your home, aren't considered household work."
- **Lender Management, LLC v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo. 2017-246** (Docket Nos. 25617-15, 25618-15, filed Dec. 13, 2017, Kerrigan, J.). The caption was read on Leagle; the opinion itself was behind a paywall. The facts and holding below come from The Tax Adviser (Aug. 2018), EY and Shearman summaries, not the opinion (Q51).

## The cases

Run on 2026-11-26 with placeholder rates: 42.25% of wages avoided, 7.65% employer payroll tax.

| Case | Tool tags | 127 | 129 | 125 | Payroll cost | Tax value to owner | Hires needed (127 / 129 / 125) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Family only (a parent, real job, market pay) | 127, 129, 125 | fail | fail | fail | 48,442 | 0 | 69 / 5 / 6 unrelated |
| Six unrelated staff at market pay | 127, 129, 125 | fail | pass | pass | 319,720 | 3,369 | 67 / 6 / 4 |
| The brother, real work at market pay | 127, 129, 125 | fail | fail | fail | 51,672 | 749 | 50 / 5 / 4 |
| A hire only to unlock the benefits | 127, 129, 125 | fail | fail | fail | 32,295 | 0 | 50 / 5 / 4 |
| A maid and a butler on the company payroll | 262, Schedule H | fail | fail | fail | 96,885 | 0 | 50 / 5 / 4 |
| Family office (three investment staff) | 162 vs 212 | fail | fail | fail | 193,770 | 2,213 | 67 / 5 / 4 |

- **Family only:** the parent passes arm's length and fails attribution, so every dollar is on the owner's side.
- **Unrelated staff:** 129 passes at 23% owner share (limit 25%), and the 55% average test passes by 42 dollars (4,167 against 4,125). That margin is thin. 125 passes at 17%. 127 needs about 67 employees taking courses for the owner's 5,250, so it isn't reachable.
- **The brother:** siblings aren't attributed under either rule, so the brother counts as a real non-owner. One hire can't carry the limits: the owner could take at most 1,667 under 129.
- **Unlock-only:** fails arm's length and substance, and the run disregards that hire in every limit (Q50).
- **Household staff:** see below.
- **Family office:** FO1 to FO4 pass and FO5 stays amber. With only three staff it still fails 129 (33%) and 125 (28%).

The tax value only counts 127 and 129. Health coverage under 106 needs no other employees, so no hire unlocks it.

The economics line in the run says the same thing twice:

- **If the hires exist only for the benefit:** the net is the tax value minus their whole payroll. That's minus 344,852 for the six-person case.
- **If the hires earn their pay:** the net is the tax value minus the benefits and plan costs for the staff. That's minus 25,131 for the same case, because the staff's own benefits cost more than the owner saves.

## Household staff

A maid or butler serving the owner's home does personal work. The cost is a personal living expense (262(a)), not the company's. If the company pays them, the payment is to the owner, as pay or a dividend (ASSUMPTION, Q53), and not a deduction. The owner is their household employer and files Schedule H. They don't count toward 127, 129 or 125, and the run gives them HA1 fail whatever the board record says.

The exception is real company work done in the home. Pub. 926 says a private secretary isn't household work. A person who does the company's administrative work is the company's employee for that work, wherever they sit, and is tested like any other hire.

## The family office (Lender Management)

Per the summaries, the Tax Court held that Lender Management carried on the trade or business of investment management under 162. The investment LLCs it managed were owned by members of the Lender families, but mostly by family members who had no interest in Lender itself. It was paid through a profits interest that paid only when the LLCs did well. It had five employees. Because the families controlled both sides, the court looked at the deal with heightened scrutiny and found it bona fide: investors could withdraw, there was no promise to keep Lender as manager forever, and the investors didn't act as one.

Mapped onto the stack, Canon Management would manage the trusts' and Foundation's investments for a fee or profits interest. Each fact becomes a check:

| Check | Lender fact | Canon Management in the twin |
|---|---|---|
| FO1 | Most of the money belongs to people who don't own the manager | The client trusts' beneficiaries, not the founder, own the managed assets |
| FO2 | Paid for services, only when results come | Fee or profits interest on results, not a return on its own capital |
| FO3 | Real salaried staff | Three staff at market pay |
| FO4 | More than an investor keeping records (Higgins) | Research, allocation, monitoring |
| FO5 | Bona fide under heightened scrutiny | **Amber.** The founder would be trustee of the client trusts and owner of the manager, so an independent co-trustee approves the management agreement (Q51) |

Lender is a trade-or-business case. It doesn't touch attribution or the owner limits, so the family office case still needs unrelated staff to pass 129 and 125. A manager that only runs the owner's own portfolio is Higgins, not Lender, and fails FO1 (self-test). A Foundation paying a disqualified manager is also a 4941 question, and Lender doesn't answer it.

## AI agents

AI agents are never employees. They aren't in the headcount and change no owner test. A thousand agents still don't unlock 127 (self-test). The run treats them only as a deductible business cost (ASSUMPTION, Q52) that raises what each real hire produces.

That moves the hiring break-even in one way: each real hire has to bring in less to earn its pay. With a placeholder 30% lift at 3,000 per hire in tools, the six-person case goes from 53,287 needed per hire to 43,298. The number of hires the limits need doesn't change.
